Identity system for Levant Noir

The Bothy Principle: the door is unlocked and the hearth is lit. Same rigour as the cold system, softer light, a ledger kept in a warm room rather than a cold one.

01

Position

Modal inheritance carried up the Atlantic coast, set down in a room with a fire in it, and answered back by electronics.

Levant Noir is the electroacoustic practice of composer and researcher Ioannis Valasakis, a multi-instrumentalist working between Eastern Mediterranean modal memory, North Atlantic coastal geography, and living electronics held in the same room. The dislocation is the subject, not a texture.

02

The Found Palette

Nothing here came out of a fan deck. Each tone was found first and specified afterwards: rowan berries against a gable wall in October, sea pinks on a shore rock in June, a limewashed distillery gable in low sun. The warmth is red, not earth; no ochre, no copper, no sand enters the system at any scale.

03

Typographic System

Three voices, all open-licence and served from Google Fonts. A variable literary serif for everything read at length, opening up at reading size and tightening for display; a humanist grotesque with a faint technical edge for structure and labelling; and a typewriter monospace for measurement, because a cask end is chalked by hand, not typeset.

04

The Bothy Principle

A bothy asks nothing of you. The door is unlocked, the hearth is laid, and there is a visitors' book in strangers' handwriting on the sill. The identity behaves the same way.

i

Unlocked, not marketed

Work is left where it can be found. No campaign architecture, no countdown.

ii

The hearth is central

Warmth carries the system; Rowan and Sea Pink do the work. Cool tones hold the structure behind them.

iii

The visitors' book

Every artefact carries a hand mark: a number, a date, an initial. Evidence of one body in one room.

iv

Weather is permitted

Deckled edges, salt-shadowed board, drifting voltage. Exposure is not damage; it is the record.

v

Nothing explained twice

Names, places and dates are stated once, in the smallest type on the page, and left to be looked up.
