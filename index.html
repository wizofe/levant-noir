Writing

Short pieces on listening.

On imperfection

The lavta’s frets are deliberately uneven, microtones set into the neck like geological strata. When I place my fingers on them I am touching the edges of several tuning systems at once. The modular synth is no different: voltage sag, thermal drift, the unpredictable bloom of feedback. These are not flaws. They are the signature of presence, the proof that sound is happening now, in this room, with these hands, this electricity.

Field recording as memory

I recorded the sound of waves in Nafplion, the call to prayer in Smyrna, the hum of cicadas by the Mediterranean. When I feed these into the Morphagene, slow them down and layer them with synthesised drones, they become something else: not a document of a place but the feeling of having been there. Sound carries memory better than photographs. It moves through you.

The space between

The pause, the silence, the negative space that gives sound its meaning: what Japanese aesthetics calls ma. In Persian music it is the tahrir, the ornament that bends between notes. In modular synthesis it is the moment when the envelope opens and you wait for the filter to bloom. Every tradition understands that music lives in the breath between phrases.

About Ioannis

I trained as a classical guitarist at the Hellenic Conservatory, took film-composition and orchestration short courses at the Guildhall School of Music & Drama, and hold a professional certificate in Composing and Producing Electronic Music from Berklee College of Music. I have a PhD in Neuroscience and am studying for the BA (Hons) Applied Music at the University of the Highlands and Islands.
