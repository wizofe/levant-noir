Statement

Levant Noir is the electroacoustic practice of composer and researcher Ioannis Valasakis. Grounded in the modal traditions of the Eastern Mediterranean and the coastal landscapes of Scotland, the work investigates how acoustic gesture, neural memory, and live signal processing converge. Rooted in the fretted microtonality of the lavta and mandolin, it deploys bespoke generative software and analogue electronics to interrogate resonance, dislocation, and the physical presence of place.

Listening room

Selected recordings and site-specific studies.

  1. Blackbirds and a lavta

    A spontaneous study in acoustic co-existence. Improvised on an Istanbul lavta using a traditional Cretan kontyliá modal framework, captured in a single unedited take beside an open window in Glasgow. The piece charts the dialogue between human rhythmic phrasing and the ambient territorial calls of urban blackbirds at dusk, refusing electronic intervention to establish the naked physical baseline of the instrument.

    Technical dossier

    lavta · single acoustic capture · open-window environmental resonance

    Glasgow · 2025 · 2:06

  2. Oud texture (Smyrna)

    Recorded in the stone interior of an Ottoman bathhouse in Izmir. A transient oud motif is refracted through real-time granular fragmentation, accompanied by processed bendir and finger cymbals. The processing acts as an acoustic mirror: short instrumental gestures are frozen into resonant micro-loops that dissolve into the natural reverberation of the stone chamber, examining how architectural space alters historical memory.

    Technical dossier

    oud → granular freeze (Beads) · percussion → multi-tap delay (Magneto) · bells → pitch-shifted reverb (Mimeophon, Clouds) · stereo field rotation via Maths

    Izmir / Smyrna · 2024 · 0:58

  3. L’ambiance

    An architectural meditation on physical and cognitive thresholds, developed for the installation Espacio Liminal (Galería Niebelungen, Berlin). The composition pairs sub-bass harmonic foundations with slow-swept narrow band-pass filters and slowed environmental recordings of glacial thaw, using deliberate voltage drift to mirror the perceptual adaptation of the ear to quiet spaces.

    Technical dossier

    Plaits (wavetable) · Ripples (band-pass, slow sweep) · Clouds (80% wet) · environmental recordings → Morphagene at half speed

    Berlin · 2024 · 3:04

  4. Lean candles

    Opening theme for an Armenian documentary on political transition. The composition constructs a dark, driving narrative around the breath and wood of the Armenian duduk, underpinned by low-frequency analogue bass synthesis and mechanical tape degradation. Down-tempo percussive structures ground the piece, allowing the modal lament of the reed to remain the primary emotional fulcrum.

    Technical dossier

    duduk → 3 s granular buffer · TR-8S rhythm architecture · Moog Sub 37 analogue bass · vinyl crackle → tape saturation → dynamic sidechain

    Documentary score · 2022 · 3:06

  5. The shearwaters (in development)

    A dual-site comparative study of pelagic seabird colonies at night: Manx shearwaters on the Atlantic cliffs of Rùm, and Scopoli's shearwaters on uninhabited Aegean islets off eastern Crete. The work interweaves nocturnal colony acoustics, subterranean nesting calls, and bowed lavta strings, interrogating the shared maritime geography that links the two distant edges of Europe.

    Technical dossier

    nocturnal field recordings · lavta · archival voice materials · multi-channel spatial staging

    Rùm · Crete · 2026

Performance · Live Set

The Travelling String: A continuous, site-responsive electroacoustic suite for lavta, mandolin, and living electronics.

Navigating from stark, unprocessed Mediterranean melodies into expansive, immersive acoustic-electronic drones, the performance uses live instrumentation as the primary control source for its own transformation. Every electronic event, from pitch-shifted grain clouds to sub-bass resonances, is derived directly from the physical touch of plectrum, fingerboard, and breath on stage.

Format: Solo performance (30–45 mins) or extended collaborative ensemble (60–75 mins). Scalable for darkened listening rooms, galleries, historic stone spaces, and experimental festivals. Full technical rider, patch schematics, and channel configurations available on request.

Writing

Critical miniatures on listening and acoustics.

On imperfection

The lavta’s frets are movable gut, deliberately displaced to accommodate microtones set into the neck like geological strata. When the fingers press between them, they encounter the friction of multiple historical tuning systems coexisting on a single fingerboard. Analogue electronics share this stubborn physicality: voltage fluctuations, component drift, the unpredictable bloom of a filter on the edge of oscillation. These are not technical failures to be calibrated away; they are the physical signature of the present moment, proof that sound is taking place in a specific room, through material that resists total control.

Field recording as palimpsest

A field recording is never an objective document of a landscape; it is a record of an encounter. In recording coastal wind at Cromarty or water inside an empty cistern in Nafplion, the microphone captures an acoustic signature that begins to decay the moment it is committed to storage. When these recordings are stretched, looped, and filtered through granular buffers, they cease to function as sonic postcards. They become acoustic palimpsests: sites where geographical fact collides with the fallibility of human memory.

The articulation of absence

In modal improvisation, a phrase is defined by what precedes and follows it. The decay of a plucked gut string leaves an acoustic vacuum that demands active listening. In modular synthesis, this same tension governs the release stage of an envelope: the space where the sound retreats and reveals the room itself. Rather than treating silence as negative space to be populated with electronic pads, the work relies on stillness as a structural weight. The music exists in the breath between gestures.

Aesthetic Lineage

The work of Levant Noir dialogues with the exploratory traditions of contemporary modal string performance and experimental sound art: the Mediterranean investigations of Michalis Siganidis and Ross Daly; the Highland and insular ecologies of Erland Cooper and Brìghde Chaimbeul; the tactile acoustic extensions of Laura Cannell and Úna Monaghan; and the analogue compositional architectures of Caterina Barbieri and Suzanne Ciani.

Biography

Ioannis Valasakis

Ioannis Valasakis (working under the performance moniker Levant Noir) is an electroacoustic composer, instrumentalist, and sound artist based in Glasgow. His practice navigates the fault lines between Mediterranean modal memory, Scottish maritime geography, and computational sound design.

Trained in classical guitar at the Hellenic Conservatory, Valasakis works primarily with traditional fretted string instruments (the Istanbul lavta and mandolin), extending their acoustic properties through modular synthesis, granular sampling, and generative algorithmic environments. His compositions are structured around the friction between physical touch and electronic instability: the microtonal drift of tied gut frets meeting voltage sag, spectral delay, and stochastic tape loops.

Valasakis holds a PhD in Neuroscience and works professionally as a research software engineer. This scientific discipline directly informs his compositional methodology, which treats acoustic memory, auditory scene analysis, and psychoacoustic perception as compositional materials rather than abstract concepts. His work incorporates site-specific field recordings and archival documentary fragments, treating the audio document as an active participant in live improvisation.

Currently conducting practice-led research in Applied Music at the University of the Highlands and Islands, his recent projects examine the acoustic ecology of the Cromarty Firth and Aegean seabird colonies, alongside live performances for multi-channel spatial systems.
